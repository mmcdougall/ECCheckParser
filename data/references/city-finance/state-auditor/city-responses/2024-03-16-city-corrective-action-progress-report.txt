Dear State Auditor Parks:

The City of El Cerrito is pleased to submit its transmittal of the required six-month Progress Report on
the City’s Corrective Action Plan in response to your Report Number 2020-803 regarding the City of El
Cerrito under the California State Auditor Local High Risk Program published on March 16, 2021. This
Corrective Action Plan Progress Report reflects continued significant improvements on the part of the
City, and has been prepared with the intent to outline the specific actions that the City of El Cerrito
has performed since our submittal of the Corrective Action Plan (CAP) in May 2021 and subsequent
Progress Report Updates in September 2021, March 2022, September 2022, March 2023, and
September 2023 to address the risk areas identified in the Report and the proposed timing for
undertaking those actions.

The City is proud to report that all recommendations have been completed and all issues have been
addressed. The City therefore respectfully requests as of this update, that the State Auditor remove
El Cerrito from the Local High Risk Program based on our substantial progress, healthy financial
condition, and fulfillment of all of the items in the identified High Risk areas.

The CAP Progress Report provides an Executive Summary and updates the matrix of State Auditor High
Risk Areas and Recommendations, the City Administration’s response to the Recommendations, and
the Corrective Action that the City has taken on the Recommendation. The City has also included
documentation for items noted in the matrix. The City has also included a final summarized list of
actions taken for each high risk area over the time period since the original report was published.

The City received comments on the City’s September 2023 CAP Progress Report from the State Auditor
team that were posted in February 2024. The City appreciates that the State Auditor recognizes the
City that has made progress in the designated risk areas, including the completion of an additional
area. This CAP Progress Report for March 2024 provides updated information on the City’s activities
that demonstrates the continued progress of the City and our firm, sustained commitment to financial
stability.

Since the submittal of the CAP Progress Report in September 2023, the City has completed the
recommendations contained within the original Report. The City has completed its annual
independent audit and received a clean audit opinion. The Annual Comprehensive Financial Report
(ACFR) for Fiscal Year 2022-23 shows an improved financial position, and the audited actuals show that
the City came in well below its expenditure targets and exceeded revenue targets, with a General Fund
surplus of $6.9 million. This surplus is a result of the continuing sustainability measures, careful budget
monitoring, being strategic about hiring for new and vacant positions, and conservative revenue
estimates.

The City adopted an updated Strategic Plan in December 2023, a result of robust community
engagement and data analysis. The Plan affirmed Fiscal Responsibility as a core value, and resulted in
five strategic goals with strategies that ensure financial stability and parallel the Fiscal Recovery and
Sustainability Plan. The City remains committed to continuous improvement and staff regularly reports
on our progress to the City Council. The City Council has continued to review the City’s budget and
financial condition through continued policy discussions and updates at their regular City Council
meetings, and there has also been increased reporting to and input from the City’s Financial Advisory
Board. Indeed, because of the improved reporting, the City Council has made budget adjustments
during the fiscal year based on analysis from our now well-staffed Finance Department and continues
to reflect conservative revenue projections and thoughtfully considered strategic expenditures for
providing service delivery within our means.

As reported in September 2023, the City Council took historic action in August 2023 to further its
commitment to long-term fiscal sustainability by investing $10 million of the City’s General Fund
reserve balance to secure funds in case of an emergency and to provide funding for the City’s pension
costs. The Council voted unanimously to place $9 million of the City’s General Fund reserve balance in
the City’s Emergency Disaster Relief Fund (EDRF) that is restricted only for use in the event of severe
economic downturns or disasters, and on September 19, 2023 established a Section 115 Pension
Funding Trust dedicated to meeting the City’s pension obligations and allocated $1 million in funding
the Trust. The Council also revised its Comprehensive Financial Policies that created a “waterfall”
priority list for allocating the year-end General Fund surplus that includes a provision for placing
additional funds in the Section 115 Trust each year. Together, these actions have affirmed the City’s
positive financial trajectory over the last three years, dramatically increasing the City’s General Fund
reserve balance and taking actions to ensure financial health and stability into the future.

Finally, the City completed its classification and compensation study. The study included a review of
all of the classifications in the City and its compensation structure and practices. Staff have analyzed
the organizational and classification structure to confirm the functions of the positions in the provision
of the City’s service delivery and will make adjustments including reclassifications and a reorganization
of divisions in the Public Works Department. As far as compensation, as expected, the study shows
that the City is below market: overall, the salary ranges are approximately 8% below market, and total
compensation is approximately 2% below market. Staff will implement the study’s recommendations
over time, as resources allow, through bargaining and other phased adjustments that will assist the
City in recruiting, motivating, and retaining competent staff while being fiscally responsible.

The City remains disappointed that the Local High Risk Dashboard, where the City had previously been
ranked in comparison with other cities, was discontinued as of October 1, 2023. The City was pleased
that as of FY 2020-21, our position had improved in the ranking list and had also improved to
“Moderate Risk” according to our score. As promised by our audit team, a new score was produced
following our September 2023 Progress Report and we learned that our score improved significantly,
from 47.29 to 61.96. However, without updated dashboard rankings nor an explanation of the
methodology (all pages relating to scoring and rankings have been removed from the website), the
public will not know what the score means nor what our actual position is compared to other cities.

City Update March 2024: The City has addressed this risk area by making continued substantial and
measurable improvements to its financial condition, and currently has the highest level of General
Fund reserves in decades.

The City has issued its FY 2022-23 Annual Comprehensive Financial Report (ACFR), which shows a
$6.9 million General Fund surplus. This was achieved in part thanks to conservative budgeting in
both revenues and expenses: in revenues, overall property and sales taxes exceeded targets and
Department-specific revenues, especially Community Development and Recreation, performed
above target as well; expenditures came in approximately 5% under budget largely due to vacant
positions creating salary savings, fiscal discipline on the part of Departments, and continued
monitoring of spending by the City Council. The ACFR presents a clean audit opinion by the City’s
independent auditor. The City’s new Finance Director has also identified that at the end of the fiscal
year, purchase order carry-overs were not fully implemented due to staffing shortages. At the
midyear budget review, this correction was made and approved by the City Council and in the future,
any carry-overs will be approved separately by the Council after the close of each fiscal year.

Now that the ACFR is completed, the Council will formally confirm allocation of the surplus per the
City’s recently revised financial policy. As previously reported, on August 15, 2023, the City Council
revised its Comprehensive Financial Policies to include a new policy that created a priority list, or
“waterfall,” for allocation for any General Fund year-end surplus. These priorities are:
             o General Fund reserve, up to the minimum target of 10% of expenditures per the
                 reserve policy;
             o Emergency Disaster Relief Fund (EDRF—restricted funds that can only be used in
                 case of severe economic downturn or a disaster), up to the minimum target of three
                 months of payroll;
             o A minimum of 33% toward pension costs (slated to be deposited in the Section 115
                 Trust);
             o Any one-time expenditures, including capital improvements, as recommended by
                 the City Manager.
The City Council has allocated an initial $1 million toward the Section 115 Trust. When the City
Council allocates the FY 2022-23 surplus to fulfill the priority list, this will include adding additional
funding to the Section 115 Trust which is expected to be a minimum of $500,000. With this policy,
in future years the City Council will allocate General Fund surplus accordingly to the Trust for pension
costs. In addition, because of the City’s healthy cash position, beginning in FY 2024-25 the City will
pre-pay the UAL cost to CalPERS instead of monthly pay-as-you-go, saving thousands in interest
costs.

Because of the actions taken by the City Council over the last two fiscal years, the City’s total General
Fund balance is $15.7 million, which is approximately 32% percent of expenditures. Of this total, $9
million is restricted as the EDRF, and $4.3 million is assigned to meet the 10% minimum reserve
target. The remaining unassigned fund balance, created as a result of the surplus General Fund
revenues, has been thoughtfully considered by the City Council for use as one-time expenditures to
address deferred maintenance and capital improvements per the policy. The Council approved
funding several maintenance projects and made a significant investment in the City’s future by

Notably, as a result of discussing and monitoring the budget regularly by receiving quarterly budget
updates, the City Council has also taken action to address the current economic conditions that have
resulted in slow revenues during this fiscal year, to avoid overspending and keep General Fund
reserves at a high level. The Council held a retreat in January to discuss priorities and kick off the
budget process. At the midyear budget review, an updated projection of the General Fund balance,
including one-time expenditures that resulted in budget amendments during FY 2023-24, was
presented to the City Council and allowed the Council and the public to see that while revenues have
been slow, our reserves remain fiscally healthy even as we are mindful of potential changes in the
economy that could require adjustments. Indeed, City staff has closely monitored Real Property
Transfer Tax (RPTT) receipts, found that an adjustment was necessary, and recommended a
decrease in the budget target for RPTT of $1 million. As a result, City staff are reviewing department
budgets monthly and are continuing to be extremely conservative regarding revenue projections.
Because of the increased review and monitoring of the budget, implementing agile budgeting
practices, and improving our analysis of revenues and expenditures, the City Council has been able
to adjust the budget accordingly and the City staff have been able to respond so that a balanced
budget will be achieved. In the coming fiscal year, revenues are expected to remain stationary, which
is a common theme among cities around the state. Therefore further action to maintain a balanced
budget will be necessary by a combination of increasing revenues and decreasing expenditures, and
the City Council along with City staff are prepared to consider all options. As a starting point, the City
is analyzing the Master Fee Schedule to ensure appropriate cost recovery, will audit Utility User
Taxes to ensure proper collections, and review vacant positions to determine if and when they
should be filled.

A critical improvement for the City has been the hiring of key staff in the Finance Department after
a prolonged staffing shortage. The City’s new Finance Director (hired in July 2023) has extensive
accounting experience, and the new Budget/Financial Services Manager (hired in September 2023)
is highly skilled in financial analysis. Additionally, the Department has filled the Senior Accountant
and one entry-level Accountant position, leaving just one vacancy. This level of experience has
enhanced the City’s financial performance, increased the quality of monitoring and reporting, and
improved the accuracy of our budget and revenue projections.

Finally, the City adopted a new Strategic Plan in December 2023, that articulates the City’s vision,
mission, and organizational values and establishes goals and strategies that will guide the work of
the organization over the next five years. Financial sustainability remains a high priority in the City’s
Strategic Plan: in the identified value of Fiscal Responsibility—we ensure responsible stewardship of
the City’s finances and assets while delivering services in the most cost-effective and efficient
manner; and in the goal of a High-Performing Organization with specific strategies to preserve and
expand revenue sources that provide sufficient resources to deliver services and programs to the
community, and to develop financing strategies for capital investments and deferred facility and
other infrastructure needs as identified in the long-range financial plan.

City Update March 2024: The Citywide classification and compensation study was completed by Koff
and Associates and presented to the City Council on March 5, 2024.

The classification section of the study reviewed and analyzed all classifications within the City and
determined proper alignment with regard to the level of scope of work performed. Every single job

description was revised, which will serve to develop a classification plan structure that enables the
City to efficiently perform the highest quality of work. In this analysis, each position was reviewed
within the Departments and by City Management to confirm the functions of the positions in the
provision of the City’s service delivery. The study resulted in thirty-two title changes and new
classifications to allow for flexibility in hiring and career growth as needs change within City
operations and programs. During the study, City Management considered the updated
classifications while assessing the current organizational structure. As an example, while reviewing
the Public Works Department, it was determined that the current Operations and Environmental
Services Division will be reorganized to better reflect the services provided and the functions
performed. This reorganization will include some reclassifications of positions and will occur during
the annual budget process, to be implemented in the next fiscal year. The entire classification plan,
which includes over 100 job descriptions, will be presented to the City Council grouped by
department/division over the next several months for approval.

The compensation section of the study confirmed what the City expected: the City’s salaries and
total compensation are below market. Salaries are behind the median by about 8%, and total
compensation is behind the median about 2%. Indeed, had the City not increased salaries through
negotiations with the bargaining units over the past two years, the City would have been further
behind. The study surveyed comparative agencies' pay and benefit structures, benchmarking 62
positions in all areas of the City. While there are a few position outliers that came in above market
for salaries, the overall picture is that the City is not currently meeting median compensation within
the survey cities. The study did find that the City’s benefits (health, retirement, social security) are
what allow us to remain somewhat competitive to recruit and retain high-quality staff, and the
consultant considers a range of 5% of the market median as competitive.

As a reminder, the entire organization did not receive a cost-of-living adjustment (COLA) in FY 2020-
21. The Management and Confidential (unrepresented) employees were frozen for the fiscal years
FY 2019-20 through FY 2021-22, the City Council imposed an additional 2% contribution into CalPERS
for a total of 3%, special pays such as auto allowances were eliminated, and this group was subject
to a total of 44 furlough days in two fiscal years resulting in a take-home pay cut of approximately
7% during that period. These actions clearly influenced the current market position of the City’s
classifications. During this fiscal year, we have been fortunate that many vacancies have been filled,
which translates into improved service delivery. However, with the current state of the economy,
inflation, and high interest rates, this study shows that it will be a challenge for the City to hire and
retain staff in the near future if we are not able to keep up with the market.

Now that the classification and compensation study is completed, the City Council and City
Management are planning how best to create and implement an equitable compensation plan that
is financially sustainable based on the recommendations in the study. This will come through
negotiations with bargaining units, and through incremental market adjustments for
Management/Confidential so long as resources allow. We recognize that this study is a tool for
management, and the data provides a baseline market survey that will give the City an instrument
to make future compensation decisions. Yet unlike the original speculation by the State Auditor team
that the study would provide areas of efficiency and cost savings, the study has instead highlighted

                                            Increasing Revenues

                    State Auditor Determination as of September 2023: Addressed.

The City continues to explore all opportunities for increasing revenues, including through fees and cost recovery,
and continuously seeks and utilizes grant opportunities to supplement revenues.
