# FAB FY 2025/2026–2026/2027 work-plan summary

## Status and source boundary

This is a compact, item-by-item summary of the Financial Advisory Board's
FY 2025/2026–2026/2027 work plan. FAB adopted the plan on January 28, 2025;
the vote appears on internal page 17. City Council later received and filed it
on March 4, 2025. The official FAB page and ArchiveCenter work-plan archive
were checked on August 30, 2026 and still identified this as the most recent
posted plan. That does not establish that no later draft or internal proposal
exists.

The primary source is the preserved ArchiveCenter copy,
`fab-work-plan-archivecenter-7932.pdf`. The corrected City Council packet
extraction is useful where the `Money Market Secure` correction matters. See
[PROVENANCE.md](PROVENANCE.md) for source URLs, hashes, and the relationship
between the two copies.

This summary describes planned work. It does not treat a planned item as
completed, a FAB motion as City Council action, or a later agenda item as an
adopted replacement plan.

## FY 2025/2026 items

1. **Budget-development revision cycles and earlier engagement** (pp. 1–2)
   — Review the existing budget timeline, identify bottlenecks and public
   engagement opportunities, consult staff and outside examples, and develop
   recommendations for City Council.

2. **Mid-year budget adjustments and unbudgeted expenditures** (pp. 2, 6–7)
   — Review proposed adjustments before Council, assess financial effects and
   alignment with approved budgets, and prepare concise recommendations. The
   plan anticipates special meetings if needed to avoid delaying Council.

3. **Service Delivery Study RFP and staffing** (p. 3)
   — Review the RFP and resulting study, assess staffing implications and
   comparable practices, and make recommendations to Council.

4. **Five-year long-term financial plan** (pp. 3–5)
   — Examine revenues, expenditures, reserve structure, and five-year trends;
   explore revenue and cost options with relevant City and community partners;
   then present proposed strategies and monitoring steps to Council.

5. **Unfunded accrued liability (UAL)** (pp. 5–6)
   — Understand CalPERS prepayment mechanics, balances, savings, risks, and
   feasibility; consult relevant experts; and prepare savings-focused options
   for Council.

6. **Quarterly budget updates** (p. 6)
   — Review quarterly updates before Council whenever practical, including
   financial variances and possible feedback.

7. **Section 115 evaluation** (p. 6)
   — Review performance, fees, return, and purpose of the Section 115 Trust.

8. **Purchasing policy and ordinance** (pp. 6–7)
   — Review the policy and ordinance, support stronger policies and internal
   controls, and make recommendations to Council as needed.

## FY 2026/2027 items

1. **Long-term financial plan and reserves** (p. 7)
   — Continue five-year-plan review, including expenditures, revenues, and
   restricted and unrestricted General Fund reserve amounts.

2. **Mid-year budget adjustments** (p. 7)
   — Continue review of adjustments and unbudgeted expenditures before Council.

3. **Service Delivery Study and staffing** (p. 8)
   — Review the study/RFP and make staffing-level recommendations after results
   are available.

4. **Comprehensive Financial Policy update** (p. 8)
   — Review and discuss the policy, including fiduciary activities, donations,
   unclaimed money, and the Money Market Secure Account as a lower-priority
   topic.

5. **Investment and capital-project policy** (pp. 8–11)
   — Evaluate the capital-project investment policy, criteria, risks,
   transparency, completed or ongoing projects, and comparable practices before
   proposing improvements.

6. **Quarterly budget updates** (pp. 11–13)
   — Obtain reports early enough for review, analyze variances and policy
   alignment, consult staff, identify risks and opportunities, and submit
   feedback to Council in advance when possible.

7. **Section 115 evaluation** (pp. 14–16)
   — Obtain trust data, analyze performance and fees, consult experts, compare
   municipal practices, identify risks and opportunities, and present
   recommendations to Council.

8. **Ongoing Comprehensive Financial Policy work** (pp. 16–17)
   — Review policy clarity and focus areas, consult staff and stakeholders,
   research comparable practices, develop prioritized recommendations, and
   prepare a Council-facing summary.
